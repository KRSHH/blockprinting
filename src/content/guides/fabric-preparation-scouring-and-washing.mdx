Most generic block printing guides borrow their advice from paper printmaking and barely mention cloth. On fabric, fiber chemistry dictates every outcome—from edge sharpness to whether your print survives its first wash.

## Choosing the Right Base Cloth
Woodblocks transfer crispest detail onto smooth, breathable **natural cellulose fibers**:

- **Cotton Cambric (60s×60s) & Poplin:** The workhorse of Bagru and Sanganer apparel. Smooth, tightly woven yarn holds fine *rekh* outlines cleanly.
- **Mulmul (Fine Muslin):** Lightweight, airy cotton ideal for scarves, summer shirts, and *doo-rokhi* double-sided printing.
- **Handloom Khadi & Slub Linen:** Slightly textured weaves that give bold *dabu* mud-resist and geometric motifs rich tactile character.
- **Avoid Polyester & Nylon Blends:** Synthetic petroleum fibers reject natural mordants and resist pastes completely.

## Scouring: Removing Invisible Factory Sizing
Even fabric labeled "ready for dyeing" often carries starch, softeners, or mill waxes. In traditional Bagru workshops, cloth is soaked and washed in **soda ash (sodium carbonate)**, **fuller’s earth (*multani mitti*)**, and a touch of castor oil, then rinsed until the water runs clear.

At home or in a small studio, hot-wash raw cotton or linen with 2 teaspoons of soda ash and a mild surfactant per kilogram of dry fabric—never use fabric softener, which coats fibers in silicone and blocks dye uptake. Press completely flat with a hot iron before pinning to your padded table.

## Curing & Fixing Color
How you fix a finished print depends on what went onto the block:

1. **Natural Mordant Prints (Alum, Iron, Madder, Indigo):** Air-dry completely, age the mordant for 24–72 hours so metallic salts lock into the tannin-prepared fiber, dye in the botanical bath, and finish with a gentle soap simmer to strip unbound dye.
2. **Water-Based Fabric Pigments:** Allow prints to cure undisturbed in open air for at least 48 hours, then heat-set with a dry iron (cotton setting, no steam) for 3–4 minutes per section using a clean press cloth.

## Long-Term Washing & Sun Care
- **First Two Washes:** Rinse separately in cool water; natural indigo and deep madder often release harmless excess surface dye ("crocking") initially.
- **Soap Choice:** Use mild, pH-neutral liquid soap. Harsh alkaline detergents and optical brighteners shift natural madder and dull indigo.
- **Shade Drying:** Hang garments inside-out in indirect shade. Direct midday ultraviolet light gradually fades botanical dyes over years.
