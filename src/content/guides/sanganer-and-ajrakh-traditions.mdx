India does not have one "block printing" tradition—it has a constellation of distinct regional clusters, each shaped by local river water minerals, available botanicals, and centuries of community specialization.

When designers treat "Indian block printing" as a single generic category, they end up asking a Bagru mud-resist workshop to imitate crisp Sanganeri florals or expecting Ajrakh geometry in a three-day turnaround. Knowing the regional map changes everything.

## Major South Asian Block Printing Clusters

| Cluster | State & Artisans | Signature Technique | Visual DNA |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bagru** | Rajasthan (*Chhipa*) | Direct natural dyes & *dabu* mud-resist | Earthy *harda* grounds, indigo, madder red, iron black, bold *jal* & geometric motifs |
| **Sanganer** | Rajasthan (*Chhipa*) | Calico direct printing & *doo-rokhi* double-sided work | Delicate florals, rosebuds, and fine *butis* on bright white grounds |
| **Ajrakhpur / Dhamadka** | Kutch, Gujarat (*Khatri*) | 14–16 step double-sided resist & mordant dyeing | Symmetrical star and architectural geometry in deep indigo, crimson, black, and white |
| **Bagh** | Madhya Pradesh (*Khatri*) | River-washed alum & iron mordant printing on cotton/silk | Stark geometric and botanical motifs in intense alizarin red and deep black on white |
| **Farrukhabad** | Uttar Pradesh | Fine multi-block *tree of life* & Persian-influenced prints | Dense interlocking florals, paisleys, and intricate border compositions |
| **Machilipatnam** | Andhra Pradesh | *Pedana* block-printed Kalamkari with buffalo milk & myrobalan | Narrative botanicals, temple borders, and rich natural vegetable palettes |

## Sanganer: Royal Florals on White
Settled just south of Jaipur in the 17th century by *Chhipa* families migrating from Gujarat, Sanganer developed under royal court patronage. Unlike Bagru’s earthy *harda*-tinted cloth, Sanganeri cotton is bleached bright white so delicate multi-color flowers, tendrils, and paisleys float cleanly across the surface. Its rarest expression, ***doo-rokhi***, prints both faces of fine muslin in mirror registration.

## Ajrakh: Sixteen Steps of Indigo and Crimson
Practiced by the *Khatri* community of Kutch and Sindh, **Ajrakh** is one of the most technically demanding textile processes on earth. A single authentic length passes through **14 to 16 distinct stages** of scouring, *harda* treatment, double-sided lime-and-gum resist (*rek*), iron black (*kat*), alum-clay resist (*gach*), repeated indigo dipping, and madder boiling. The finished cloth carries crisp geometric symmetry on both sides simultaneously.

## Bagh and Farrukhabad
In **Bagh** (Madhya Pradesh), the mineral-rich water of the Baghini River historically gave alum-mordanted madder reds an extraordinary fiery intensity against stark iron black. In **Farrukhabad**, master carvers perfected dense, multi-block Persian-inspired *kairi* (paisley) and *Tree of Life* textiles that shaped European chintz trade for centuries.
