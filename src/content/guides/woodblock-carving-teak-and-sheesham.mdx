Every hand block print begins in a quiet woodcarving shop long before dye ever touches water. In Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh, block carving (*khanda* work) is its own hereditary discipline—separate from printing and dyeing—centered historically in Jaipur, Pethapur (Gujarat), and Farrukhabad.

## Choosing and Seasoning the Timber
A printing block must endure tens of thousands of damp strikes without splintering or warping. Two South Asian hardwoods dominate workshop practice:

- **Teak (*Sagwan*):** The gold standard for fine floral and geometric work. Its natural oleoresins resist water damage, and its tight, straight grain holds crisp 1 mm ridges cleanly.
- **Sheesham (Indian Rosewood):** Dense, durable, and widely used for bold background (*datta*) and resist (*dabu*) blocks.
- **Pearwood & Boxwood:** Historical European equivalents valued for similar fine-grained density.

Timber planks are air-seasoned for months, planed dead-flat to roughly 5–7 cm thickness, and rubbed with white chalk paste so pencil or charcoal tracings read clearly across the wood face.

## From Tracing to Carved Relief
The carver transfers the pattern in reverse onto the chalked surface—often pricking a master paper tracing and dusting charcoal powder (*pouncing*) through the pinholes. Using a set of thirty or more narrow steel chisels, gouges, and drills (*farma*, *chheni*, *barma*) tapped with a small wooden mallet, the carver excavates the negative space 4 to 6 mm deep.

For razor-thin Sanganeri dots or hairlines that timber alone cannot hold, carvers hammer **thin brass strips and pins** directly into the wood face—a hybrid technique known as *pittal* work.

## Air Vents, Handles, and Registration Pins
Three engineering details separate a real workshop block from a souvenir stamp:

1. **Ergonomic Handle:** Carved directly from a solid back block or pegged cleanly on top so the printer’s palm can strike squarely for hours without blistering.
2. **Air Release Vents:** Small cylindrical holes drilled vertically through deep background cavities prevent suction from lifting wet dye paste back off the fabric.
3. **Registration Notches (*Gudh*):** Tiny corner pins carved outside the main motif that tell the printer exactly where the next repeat lands.

## Conditioning and Caring for Blocks
Brand-new teak blocks are soaked or brushed generously with **mustard or sesame oil** for several days so the wood fibers saturate before touching wet mordant paste. After a printing run, blocks are scrubbed gently with a soft coir brush in cool water, dried flat in the shade—never in direct midday sun, which splits the grain—and lightly re-oiled before storage.
