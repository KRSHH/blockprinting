Unlike synthetic pigments that sit on top of fabric with a plastic binder, traditional natural dyes bond chemically inside the fiber. That gives block-printed cotton and linen their signature soft hand-feel and luminous depth that ages gracefully over decades.

## Why Mordants Matter on Cotton and Linen

Cellulose fibers (cotton, linen, hemp, khadi) have a lower natural affinity for most botanical dyes than protein fibers like silk or wool. To achieve crisp, washfast prints, artisans print **mordant pastes** directly onto tannin-prepared cloth, then immerse the entire fabric in a heated dye bath. Color fixes only where the mordant was stamped.

### Core Traditional Mordants

- **Alum (Potassium Aluminum Sulfate):** Produces brilliant madder reds, warm marigold yellows, and clear botanical tones.
- **Iron (Ferrous Sulfate / Fermented Jaggery-Iron):** Shifts madder into deep charcoal black or plum, and deepens tannins into slate and espresso tones.
- **Myrobalan (*Harda*):** A tannin-rich fruit powder used as a pre-mordant that prepares cellulose to bind alum and iron cleanly.

## Natural Thickeners for Crisp Edges

Watery dye runs along fabric threads by capillary action. To hold razor-sharp carved woodblock detail, mordants and dyes are blended with natural gums:

1. **Guar Gum:** Hydrates rapidly in warm water and washes out cleanly after curing.
2. **Gum Arabic:** Ideal for fine Sanganeri outlines where ultra-clean definition is required.
3. **Tamarind Seed Powder:** Widely used in Rajasthan and Kutch for both mordant pastes and mud-resist (*dabu*) printing.

## Signature Botanical Color Palette

- **Deep Blue (Indigo):** Achieved via fermented natural indigo (*Indigofera tinctoria*) vats, often paired with clay-and-gum *dabu* resist printing.
- **Crimson & Terracotta (Madder Root):** *Rubia cordifolia* or *Rubia tinctorum* boiled gently with alum-printed cloth yields rich brick red; where iron was printed, the same bath turns jet black.
- **Ochre & Gold (Pomegranate Rind & Marigold):** High-tannin pomegranate skins produce warm, lightfast mustard golds.

## Finishing for Long-Term Washfastness

After dyeing, rinse thoroughly in cool running water and simmer briefly in a mild pH-neutral soap bath to remove unbound dye molecules and residual thickener. Dry in indirect sunlight to preserve the natural richness of botanical pigments.
